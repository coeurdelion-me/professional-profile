# Platform facts

Read this whenever you are about to state a platform setting, limit or behaviour. Each fact carries the date it was last checked against the vendor's own pages. Platforms change these without notice, so tell the person to confirm anything that looks different on their screen, and follow what they see over what this file says.

## LinkedIn (checked 17 August 2026)

- Character limits. LinkedIn's own help pages state no character count for the headline or the About section. The figures in circulation come from member posts rather than from LinkedIn. The profile editor shows a live counter as the person types, and that counter is the only number to write to.
- Open to work. The setting is separate from anything on the visible profile and offers three visibilities. All LinkedIn members: adds the green photo frame, which their employer can see. Recruiters only: shows the signal to holders of a recruiter licence and adds no frame. The third keeps it private and only tailors their job recommendations.
- The honest limit of recruiters only: LinkedIn says it takes steps to keep the signal from recruiters at the person's own employer and does not guarantee it. It removes the public frame and reduces the risk rather than removing it.

## Applicant tracking systems (checked 17 August 2026)

- Portals state which file formats they accept and almost never state which one parses best.
- The size they accept can be far larger than the size their parser reads. One major applicant tracking system documents accepting uploads up to 100 MB while failing to parse anything above 2.5 MB, so a file can upload cleanly and be read as blank, with nothing shown to the candidate.
- The layout faults that break parsing hardest: contact details in a header, footer or text box; a two-column layout; graphics or photographs; tables used for layout.

# Market: continental Europe, in English

Use from Phase 0 when they are writing in English for a European employer outside the UK. The kit writes in English only. Practice varies more between European countries than within the UK or the US, so ask which country and which employer, and where the employer states what it wants, follow the employer.

- The document is a CV.
- Length: two to three A4 pages is common, with more detail on education and skills than a UK or US reader expects. Keep the two-page default for senior and board roles, where restraint still reads as seniority.
- Photograph: common and often expected in parts of Europe, and unusual in others. Ask what the target country and employer expect rather than assuming, and leave it off where there is no clear expectation.
- Personal details: nationality or the right to work in the country is often included and useful. Age and marital status are increasingly left off; include them only where the employer asks.
- Languages: list each language with its level. Where they know the Common European Framework level, such as C1, use it, because European readers recognise it at once.
- Europass: the European Union's standard CV format, and voluntary. Offer it only where the employer or an institution asks for it, since most employers accept an ordinary CV.
- Spelling: British or Irish English unless the employer's own advertisement uses American English, in which case match the employer.
- Dates: month and year for roles, such as March 2024, and the day first where a full date is needed, such as 4 March 2024. Avoid all-numeric dates.
- Paper size: A4.
- Style reference, where the person has no conventions of their own: the English Style Guide of the European Commission's Directorate-General for Translation, for plain English as European institutions write it.

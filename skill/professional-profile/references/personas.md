# Review panels

Use at Phases 5, 5b, 6 and 7.

## The review method
Review each assembled draft through the relevant personas, from the router below. For each HUMAN persona, give three things and no more:
1. A verdict, in one of two words: shortlist, or not-yet.
2. The single line, bullet or section that most drove that verdict, quoted.
3. One specific change, naming what to cut, add or rewrite, and where.

A human persona review with no quoted line and no named change has not happened, whatever it says.

The ATS screener is exempt from that format, and the exemption is deliberate rather than an oversight. It is a parser rather than a reader, so it has no opinion to attribute to a quoted line. It gives the verdict, shortlist or not-yet, and then the two lists its entry in the router specifies: the formatting breaches, each named with its location, and the three keyword lists. Do not compress those lists into one change to keep the shape of the others.

Revise against the changes. Then show the revision to every persona that returned not-yet, and to no others, and have each say in one line whether it now shortlists and what still stops it if it does not.

## The router
Always include the Honest friend. Add the personas for every track they chose, primary and secondary.
- A, permanent role: Recruiter, Hiring manager, Honest friend
- B, career change: Hiring manager, Sector insider, Honest friend
- C, portfolio, board or non-executive: Board recruiter, Chair or CEO, Honest friend
- D, interim or fractional: Recruiter, Hiring manager, Honest friend
- E, independent advisory: The buyer, Sector insider, Honest friend

For the bio at Phase 5b on tracks A and B only, replace the track panel with the Honest friend and the Introducer. The recruiter and the hiring manager never receive a bio, so on those two tracks they would be judging the document against a use it does not have. Tracks C, D and E keep their own panels for the bio, because a board recruiter, a chair and a buyer all read one.

The ATS screener is added by ROUTE rather than by track, matching the route rule in `references/cv.md`. Add it whenever the person says they will apply through an online portal or a large employer's careers site, whichever track they are on, including an interim role advertised through an agency portal. Leave it out when they are approached by a search firm, a chair, or someone who already knows their name. If they will use both routes, run it, and say which version of the CV is for which route.

The ATS screener runs in Phase 5 and nowhere else. No applicant tracking system parses a bio or a LinkedIn profile, so a keyword and formatting review of either produces findings that no reader will ever act on. Leave it out of Phases 5b and 6 whatever the route.

## The personas
- Recruiter or headhunter. Scans for thirty seconds. Looks for clear level, fit, evidence of impact, and no red flags. Rejects vague, generic, no numbers. Always asks: would I put this in front of my client?
- Hiring manager. Looks for whether this person can do the job and whether the claims are believable. Rejects responsibility dressed up as achievement. Always asks: do I want this person in my team on Monday?
- ATS screener, the machine. A keyword and parsing filter rather than a reader. It has two jobs and both produce a list rather than an opinion. FORMATTING: check standard section headings, no text inside images or text boxes, no layout tables, common fonts, ordinary reverse-chronological order, dates in a consistent format, and no critical information in a header or footer. Name each breach and where it is. KEYWORDS: work from the real job advertisement or role specification found at Phase 1. Pull the terms it repeats, including the exact wording of qualifications, tools, standards and job titles. Then produce three lists: terms present in the CV, terms absent that the person can honestly claim, and terms absent that they cannot. Add only the middle list, in their own sentences rather than as a keyword strip, because a machine scores the term and a human then reads the sentence around it. Say plainly when a term cannot be claimed, since that is a gap to address rather than a word to insert. Always asks: can I parse this, and does it match the brief?
- Sector insider. A respected peer in the field. Looks for credibility and the right reference points. Rejects surface knowledge that would not survive a follow-up question. Always asks: does this person actually know the field?
- Board recruiter. Looks for governance exposure, committee specifics and fiduciary credibility. Rejects an executive CV with the word board sprinkled over it and no real evidence. Always asks: can this person hold a board to account, rather than just sit on one?
- Chair or CEO. Looks for judgement, sector fit, and what this person adds to the board's composition. Rejects ego and narrowness. Reads the disagreement answer closely, because a non-executive who cannot disagree well is worse than an empty seat. Always asks: will this person make my board better?
- The buyer, for advisory tracks. Looks for a clear problem solved and a reason to choose this person over a firm. Rejects generalist mush. Always asks: why this person, and why now?
- The Introducer, for the bio on tracks A and B. Someone who has been handed the bio and has to introduce this person to a room or in an email, having never met them. Works only from what is on the page and cannot ask a follow-up question. Rejects a bio that needs the first paragraph plus the third before it makes sense, and jargon that lands only inside the person's own sector. Always asks: can I introduce this person in two sentences from this, and will the room know who they are?
- Honest friend, always. Looks for over-claiming, hidden strengths, the thing being avoided, and anything that sounds AI-written. Rejects fluff, false modesty and false confidence. Always asks: is this actually true, and does it sound like you?

# The CV: structure, register, route and delivery

## Phase 3: Output mode and template
Establish which output mode they want:
- A, Library: you suggest an archetype below, by their level and track, and draft into it
- B, Bring your own: they paste the format or template they want, and you write copy that fits it exactly
- C, Content only: you give them the words, bullets and phrasing, and they place it into their own document

For Library mode, pick the CV archetype by seniority and situation rather than by job title. When in doubt, ask.

1. Board and non-executive. Lead with a short profile stating the level and the kind of governance value, without adjectives. Then a governance section carrying board and committee roles with the organisation's type and scale, because a reader is placing you in an appointment market before they read anything else. Then executive experience, framed as scope and judgement rather than tasks. Restraint signals seniority: fewer and larger claims, no buzzwords. Two pages, and a third only where the portfolio genuinely needs it.
2. Interim, fractional and independent advisory. Lead with the problem you are hired to fix and the speed of fixing it. Each engagement: the situation on arrival, what you did, the state on exit, and the duration. Day rate or retainer model where they want it visible. This shape and shape 1 read differently on purpose: a board reader wants governance and judgement, an interim buyer wants a problem solved and a clock.
3. Executive and senior leadership. Profile, then a short selected-achievements block, then chronological experience. Every role: scope (team size, budget, remit) then two to four outcome bullets. Quantify leadership impact rather than activity.
4. Mid-career professional or specialist. Tight profile, then chronological experience with outcome-led bullets. A skills section only where the field expects keyword matching. Lead each role with the result and support it with the method.
5. Career changer. Skills-and-evidence forward: group transferable strengths with proof, then chronology underneath. Name the target explicitly. Address the obvious gap head on.
6. Early career or graduate. Education and capability forward, experience close behind, including internships, projects and volunteering. Concrete examples rather than claims of potential. One page.

CV rules for all archetypes: one to two pages for most, a third only at genuine portfolio seniority. Reverse chronological within each section. Bullets are outcomes rather than duties, strong verb first and result last. Quantify wherever it is honest. Consistent tense and formatting, no orphaned half-lines.

Length and personal details follow the market's locale file, which overrides the page counts above where the two differ, because a length that reads as senior restraint in one market reads as thin in another.

On applicant tracking systems, and who decides whether they matter. The rule is the route, not the seniority. Applying through an online portal or a large employer's careers site: assume a machine reads it first, so use standard section headings, no text inside images or text boxes, no layout tables, common fonts, ordinary reverse-chronological structure, and mirror the target role's language where it is true. Approached by a search firm, a chair, or someone who already knows your name: no machine is involved, so optimise for the human reader instead. Board and non-executive appointments usually run through search firms and networks, so where that is the route, track C can skip the machine-readability rules entirely. Apply the route rule rather than the track even here: a track C person answering a public appointment listing or any other online portal is on the machine route like anyone else, and gets the rules and the screener. Say so plainly rather than leaving them to guess, because a board CV written for a filter that never reads it is a wasted page. Record the route in the working record.

Where the machine does read it, the keyword work is the ATS screener's job in Phase 5 and it runs off the real advertisement or role specification from Phase 1 rather than off a generic list. Ask for that document now if they have not produced it, because keyword matching without it is guesswork. The same rule decides whether the ATS screener joins the panel, as `references/personas.md` sets out.

One more choice, and it belongs here rather than after the draft. The CV has no personal voice by design, but it does have a register, and people disagree about it more often than about structure. Offer two: PLAIN FACTUAL, which states scope and outcome with no colour and reads as senior restraint, or SLIGHTLY WARMER, which keeps the same facts and allows a little more connective language in the profile. Write the same two-line profile in both, show them side by side, and let them choose. Record the choice in the working record, because a register argued about at Phase 5 costs a redraft.

## Phase 5: Drafting the CV
Draft the CV from the real material gathered at intake and the locked positioning line, applying the writing rules in `references/writing.md` and `references/ai-tells.md` throughout. The structure comes from the output mode chosen at Phase 3. In Library mode, draft into the archetype you agreed there. In bring-your-own mode their template supplies the structure, so fit it exactly and do not substitute an archetype for it. In content-only mode there is no document to structure, so write the sections as text they can place themselves, and label each one so they know where it goes.

Write in the CV register they chose at Phase 3, and read it off the working record rather than assuming. PLAIN FACTUAL states scope and outcome with no colour. SLIGHTLY WARMER carries the same facts with a little more connective language, and it reaches the profile only: the bullets stay factual under either choice, because that is what makes them defensible. If no register was recorded, ask which one before drafting rather than picking for them.

Deliver the CV one section at a time as "Keeping replies deliverable" describes, then run the panel over the assembled document rather than over each section as it lands. A thirty-second scan is a judgement about a whole CV, so a verdict on a fragment is not the thing being simulated.

Then review the assembled draft through the relevant personas by the review method in `references/personas.md`, revise as that method describes, and show the revised draft.

## Phase 8: Delivering the CV
For the CV, start with the upload screen, but tell them what it will and will not tell them. It lists the formats it accepts. It almost never says which one parses best, and the accepted-size limit it shows can be far larger than the size the parser behind it can actually read, so a file can upload cleanly and be read as blank. `references/platform-facts.md` carries the documented case. Pick from the accepted list, keep the file well under a couple of megabytes, and keep the text selectable rather than flattened into an image. Three layout faults break parsers hardest, and they go beyond the machine-readability list at Phase 3 rather than repeating it: their name and contact details sitting in a header, footer or text box rather than in the body, a two-column layout, and graphics or photographs. Say these three even where the Phase 3 rules were already applied, because that list covers headings, images, tables and fonts and names none of them. Name the file with their own name and the role or organisation, so it survives a folder of attachments. If Phase 5 produced two versions for two routes, say which is which and keep the file names different, because the wrong file sent to a portal is the failure this prevents.

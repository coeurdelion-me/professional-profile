# Writing rules

Use at Phases 5, 5b and 6, for every document.

Write in plain language, judged by what the reader can do with the text rather than by how simple it looks. Four tests, from the international plain language standard (ISO 24495-1): it is relevant, because it carries what the named reader needs and nothing they do not; it is findable, because the strongest signal sits where that reader looks first; it is understandable, because it uses words the reader uses, one idea per sentence; and it is usable, because the reader can act on it, with the contact details and next step in plain sight.

Follow the market's locale file for spelling, date format and the style reference. Where the person has conventions of their own, theirs win.

Avoid AI tells. Selectors notice them and they read as effort outsourced. The patterns to keep out, and how to fix them, are in `references/ai-tells.md`.

CV-specific rules: bullets are outcomes rather than duties, verb first and result last. Quantify, because one real number beats any adjective. No editorialising. No first person and no "I", the subject is implied. Consistent tense: present for the current role, past for previous ones. Cut adjectives doing no work, since "successfully managed" is just "managed".

Universal: every claim has to be defensible in an interview, so cut what cannot be backed. Specific beats grand. Read it aloud, and if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.

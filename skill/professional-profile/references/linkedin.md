# LinkedIn

Use at Phase 6.

## Structure
LinkedIn structure: first person, warmer than the CV, the same facts. The headline does more than state a job title: who you help and how, or the level and the value, and it has to fit on a phone screen. About opens with the real thing rather than a warm-up line, three or four short paragraphs, first person, reading like the person talking. Experience is lighter than the CV, outcome-led, scannable. Featured items and skills only where they serve the target audience. The captured voice from Phase 4 lives in the About section: the experience entries take a lighter version of it, and the headline is too short to carry a voice and is written for clarity instead. The CV can be neutral. The About cannot, or it dies on the screen.

## Phase 6: LinkedIn
This is the only output that changes in public the moment it is saved, so settle visibility before drafting anything. A CV and a bio go to a reader they choose. A profile is seen by their current employer, their colleagues and their clients, and a headline announcing availability is an announcement whether or not they meant it as one.

Ask three things and take the answers as constraints on the draft rather than as background. Does anyone at their current employer know they are looking, and does the answer change what the headline can say? Which parts can change now and which have to wait, since a rewritten About draws far less attention than a changed headline or job title. Do they want to signal availability at all yet, or privately for now? LinkedIn's Open to work setting is separate from anything on the visible profile, so where the answer is "not publicly yet", point them at it rather than at a coded headline, which reads as coded to everyone including their employer. Tell them what each of its visibility options does, and the honest limit of the one that hides the signal from other members, from `references/platform-facts.md`, because the middle option is the one a person still in post will pick and they should decide with its limit in front of them. Confirm the option names from the live setting, since these are the labels rather than a promise about what LinkedIn calls them next year. Record the three answers in the CONSTRAINTS section of the working record.

Then sequence the changes rather than delivering them as one edit. Give them an order to apply the work in, and say which single change is the loudest, so a person still in post can start with the quiet parts.

Draft in this order, waiting after each, per "Keeping replies deliverable": the headline, then the About, then the experience entries one role at a time. Output mode does not apply here. Modes A, B and C choose a CV's structure, and a profile's structure belongs to the platform.

- The headline. Written for clarity in a search result rather than for voice, because it is too short to carry one. Who they help and how, or the level and the value.
- The About. First person, warmer, opening with the real thing rather than a warm-up line, three or four short paragraphs, readable on a phone, ending by inviting rather than instructing. This is where the captured voice from Phase 4 lives and where it is judged.
- The experience entries. Lighter than the CV, outcome-led and scannable, and warmer than the CV without adopting the About's full voice. Carry the same roles the CV carries, and where the profile already runs further back, leave those earlier entries in place with a line each rather than deleting history a reader may have seen. Every role appearing in both has to agree on title, dates and scope, which is what the cross-check at Phase 7 tests.
- Featured and Skills. Only what serves the named target audience. Say what to remove as well as what to add, since an unpruned skills list dilutes the ones that matter.

On length: the headline and the About each have a character limit set by the platform, and both are shorter than people expect. `references/platform-facts.md` records what LinkedIn does and does not publish about them. The profile editor shows a live character counter as they type. Ask them to read it off the field and write to it. Never assume a number, and never let a draft overrun a limit you have not checked.

Run the relevant personas by the review method in `references/personas.md`. Revise. Show.

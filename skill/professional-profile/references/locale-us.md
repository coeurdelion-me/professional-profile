# Market: United States

Use from Phase 0 when they are writing for the US market. These are the norms of US careers guidance rather than law, and practice varies by sector and employer, so where an employer states what it wants, follow the employer.

- The document is a resume. In the US a CV usually means a longer academic record, so use "resume" in everything you write for them unless they are applying in academia or research.
- Length: one page early in a career and two pages for most experienced people. A third page reads as padding to most US readers, so hold even senior candidates to two unless the role asks for a full record.
- Personal details: name, phone number, email address, city and state, and a LinkedIn address. Leave off a photograph, age, date of birth and marital status. US employers avoid them because they invite discrimination claims, and a resume carrying them reads as unfamiliar with the market.
- Spelling: American English, such as organize, program and color.
- Dates: month and year for roles, such as March 2024. Avoid all-numeric dates, because a US reader takes the month first and most other readers take the day first.
- Paper size: US Letter.
- Style reference, where the person has no conventions of their own: the federal plain language guidance published on digital.gov, which replaced plainlanguage.gov, for plain language; the Chicago Manual of Style for mechanics such as capitalisation and numbers.

# Track C: portfolio, board or non-executive follow-ups

Use at Phase 1 when track C is among the tracks they chose.

This is the longest follow-up set in the kit, because board appointment turns on specifics that a general CV conversation never reaches. It runs in TWO PARTS with a gate between them, and the gate is a real stopping point: part 1 is recall, which they can answer from memory and their CV in one sitting, and part 2 asks them to think and to look one document up. Offer to stop at the gate and resume later, because a considered answer to part 2 beats a tired one.

Part 1, the record. Ask each in turn:
- What kind of organisation, sector and stage do you want to govern?
- Listed, private, private-equity backed, charity, public sector, or a mix? These are different appointment markets and the CV differs for each.
- What is your actual board exposure, including the informal kind: full board roles, committee membership, trustee roles, advisory boards, and reporting to a board as an executive?
- Which committees, specifically: audit, remuneration, nomination, risk, or another? Name the committee and what you did on it. Chairing a committee is a stronger and separate claim from sitting on one.
- Have you sat through a board evaluation, and have you been on the receiving end of one?
- Any regulated-sector exposure, and which regulator?

Summarise the record back in six lines or fewer, update the working record with Next set to "Phase 1, part 2 of 2", and tell them part 2 is six questions of judgement plus one piece of homework, which is finding a real board role specification. Offer them the choice of carrying straight on or picking it up next time. If they stop, give them the working record and tell them to paste it back to resume. This gate is a STOP like any other.

Part 2, what you bring. Ask each in turn:
- What gap in a board's skills matrix do you fill? Chairs recruit to a gap rather than to the best CV in the pile, so name it in their language: cyber, ESG, remuneration, turnaround, international expansion, digital, or whatever yours is.
- Why do you want a board seat? Push on this one, meaning push past the stock answer rather than past an honest one. "Building a portfolio career" says what they want to get rather than what they bring, and it is the answer that filters people out. Keep going until the answer says what the board gets, and if they have not worked it out yet, treat that as the not-yet-decided case in "When the person does not know" rather than as evasion. Chairs ask this question too, so a person who cannot answer it here has found something worth knowing before an interview finds it for them.
- How do you behave when you disagree with a chief executive you respect? Ask for a real occasion, what they did, and what happened. This is close to the only thing a chair wants to know about a new non-executive.
- What time commitment can you give, in days per year, and how many boards do you want in total?
- Any conflicts of interest, current or likely: competing directorships, shareholdings, family or client relationships?
- Are you offering oversight, a specific functional expertise, or both?
- The homework: find one real board role specification, from a search firm, a charity trustee advertisement or a public appointment listing. What three requirements does it name that you can clearly evidence? Board specifications are usually more explicit than job advertisements about the gap being filled, so this discipline works better here than anywhere else in the kit. If they have not got one to hand, say what to search for and offer to wait rather than moving on without it.
